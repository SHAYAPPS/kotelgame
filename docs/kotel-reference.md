# Western Wall (Kotel) Plaza: greybox reference sheet

Factual reference for building Mission 1's 1:1 greybox. Units are meters.

**How to read this sheet**
- **[S]** means the figure is sourced (see *Sources*). Where sources disagree, a range is given.
- **[E]** means it is an estimate. The basis is given each time, usually proportions in photos and
  aerial views, arithmetic from sourced areas, or typical construction. Check [E] values against
  Google Earth (its ruler tool) before treating them as final.
- Research note: when this sheet was written, direct page fetches (Wikipedia, thekotel.org and others)
  were blocked, so the figures come from search-engine summaries of the listed pages. Treat
  single-source numbers with care.

## 2. Stone courses (bottom to top)

Sources agree on the order of the bands and on 7 Herodian + 4 early Islamic courses. For the top band
they give **16** (Heritage Foundation, Hebrew) or **17** (English Wikipedia) courses. Visible courses
in total: **27-28**.

| Band | Courses | Typical block | Approx. span above floor | Look |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Herodian** (late 1st c. BCE) | **7** [S] | Course height ~1.0-1.1 m [S]. Length avg ~3 m (1-4 m common; some to ~12 m) [S]. Weight 2-8 t [S] | **0 → ~7.5 m** [E: 7 x ~1.05 m] | Huge ashlars laid dry (no mortar). Each has a **drafted margin ~8 cm wide, sunk ~2 cm** around a flat, slightly raised face (boss) [S]. Each course is set back a couple of centimeters from the one below (a slight batter) [E, the usual description of Herodian walls]. |
| **Early Islamic / Umayyad** (7th-8th c.) | **4** [S] | "Medium", plainly dressed, no margins [S]. ~0.8-1.0 m high, ~1-1.5 m long [E, from photos: about 3/4 of a Herodian course] | **~7.5 → ~11 m** [E] | Flatter, rougher faces; more weathered joints. |
| **Mamluk / Ottoman** (13th-19th c.; the top few are 19th c.) | **16-17** [S] | Small squared blocks, ~0.4-0.5 m high, ~0.5-0.8 m long [E: ~8 m of height / 16-17 courses] | **~11 → ~19 m** [E] | Small regular blocks in mortar; many courses. |
| Below the floor | 17 [S] | Herodian | down to bedrock | Seen only in the tunnels and in excavations. |

The biggest known Herodian stone, the "Western Stone", is ~13.6 m long x 3.3 m high. It sits north of
Wilson's Arch, inside the tunnels, **not** in the plaza [S].

**Surface details**
- **Color:** Jerusalem limestone, pale cream to honey. The lower Herodian blocks are weathered grey-beige
  with dark patches and a polish where hands touch them; the upper small stones are lighter and more
  yellow [E, photos].
- **Plants:** tufts growing in the joints of the upper and middle courses, mostly caper
  (*Capparis spinosa*); the plant usually identified as biblical hyssop also grows there [S].
  Conservators check the wall about twice a year and inject grout into cracks caused by weathering and
  plants [S].
- **Notes (kvitlach):** folded paper packed into the joints from ~0.3 m up to ~2.5 m (arm reach) [E].
  They are cleared twice a year (before Passover and Rosh Hashanah) [S].
- Pigeons, and swifts in spring [E, commonly photographed].

## 4. Back fence and hand-washing stations

- **Back fence** between the prayer area and the open (upper) plaza: a **low barrier ~1.0-1.2 m** high,
  made of stone-clad posts with metal railings and portable metal crowd barriers [E, photos]. It runs
  N-S roughly parallel to the wall at ~30 m [E].
- **Openings** [E]: one gap into the men's section near its north/center and one into the women's
  section near the south, each ~3-5 m wide. Staff hand out paper kippot at the men's opening and
  shawls/skirts at the women's opening. Mark them as gaps in the fence.
- **Netilat yadayim stations** [S: they exist and are on the plaza; layout E]: stone or steel troughs
  about waist height (~0.9 m), each ~3-6 m long, with a row of taps (~6-10). **Two-handled washing
  cups** (natla) hang from the taps on chains. They stand on the plaza side of the back fence, near the
  men's and women's openings. The Heritage Foundation installed new sinks in recent years [S]. Also
  drinking fountains [S].

## 8. Southern entrance and security checkpoint (Dung Gate)

- **Dung Gate** is in the southern Old City wall and is the gate closest to the Kotel [S]. A new southern
  entrance to the plaza was built in 1988 [S].
- The **checkpoint** is between Dung Gate and the south-west corner of the plaza [S/E]. Walk-through
  **metal detectors** and **bag X-ray machines** [S], in a stone/steel **pavilion with a flat canopy**
  and 2-4 lanes plus a manual-search lane [E]. There are checks at every entrance [S]. On Shabbat,
  observant Jews can ask for a manual search instead [S].
- **Path** [E]: Dung Gate → north ~100-200 m across the open area inside the gate (buses, drop-off,
  the Davidson Center/archaeological park on the east) → checkpoint → down steps/ramps into the south
  end of the upper plaza, near where the Mughrabi Bridge starts. The south-east corner of the plaza is
  where the women's section, the bridge and the fenced archaeological area meet.

## 9. Other level-design notes

- **Paving:** large rectangular Jerusalem-stone slabs (~0.6 x 0.4 to 1.0 x 0.5 m, pale grey-cream), worn
  smooth, with drainage lines [E].
- **Skyline above the wall (looking east):**
  - **Dome of the Rock:** the gold dome is visible to the **east-north-east**, **~150-200 m** away
    [S: ~150 m quoted; E: direction]. Dome diameter **20.2 m** and height **20.5 m** [S]. It tops out
    **~35 m above the plaza floor** as quoted [S] (a ~35 m total building height on its raised platform
    gives ~55 m in game coordinates [E]; use the lower value if the dome shows too much).
  - **Chain Gate (Bab al-Silsila) minaret** (Mamluk, 1329): on the wall line just **north of
    Wilson's Arch** [S], **~29-35 m tall** [S]. It rises from Temple Mount level [E]. A square shaft
    changing to an octagon, then a small dome.
  - **Al-Aqsa mosque** grey lead dome to the **south-east** [E]. The **Fakhriyya minaret** is near the
    south-west corner of the Temple Mount, above the Mughrabi Gate area [E].
- **Sun (Jerusalem, 31.8° N):** the wall faces west, so **the wall face and prayer area are in shade in
  the morning** (the sun rises behind the Temple Mount). The face gets direct light from about midday
  and glows gold in the late afternoon [E, geometry]. Noon sun elevation is ~81° in June and ~35° in
  December [E, computed]. For a calm morning shift: sun low in the east behind the wall, long shadows
  across the prayer area, and the upper plaza and western buildings lit.
- **Crowds:** a weekday morning has dozens to a few hundred people: minyanim of men in tallit and
  tefillin, tourists, soldiers (swearing-in ceremonies are held here), bar-mitzvah groups with drums
  near the back fence, and police at the entrances [E]. Holidays bring tens of thousands [E].
- **Cover and gameplay:** the useful solid cover is the back fence, the mechitza, the stone bookcases,
  the washing troughs, the terrace steps, the Wilson's Arch piers and the bridge trestles. The bridge
  and the western rooftops (Aish HaTorah, Porat Yosef) are the high ground.

## Coordinate proposal

Proposal: the game's +X is **east**, +Y is **up**, and **+Z is south** (three.js is right-handed, so with
x = east and y = up, -Z points north; yaw 0 therefore looks north along the wall). The wall face is the
plane **x = 0**, and the plaza is at **x < 0**. The prayer floor is **y = 0**. The prayer section is
centered on **z = 0**. The wall is aligned to the grid (the ~7° rotation is ignored).
